Cryptographic Teardown: XChaCha20 vs AES-256

The foundational difference between NordPass and 1Password lies in their chosen mathematical cipher:

Why NordPass chose XChaCha20: While standard AES-256 is the legacy enterprise standard, it relies on dedicated hardware execution units (AES-NI). On mobile chipsets, IoT devices, or microprocessors without dedicated hardware acceleration, AES can experience cache-timing vulnerabilities. XChaCha20 uses a massive 192-bit nonce, practically eliminating nonce reuse risk, and computes up to 3x faster on mobile devices with zero hardware timing vulnerabilities.

1Password’s Secret Key Architecture: 1Password combines an AES-256 key derived from your master password with a locally generated 128-bit Secret Key. This creates an exceptional barrier against brute-force attacks even if 1Password’s encrypted cloud blobs were compromised. However, the requirement to preserve and enter the Secret Key on every new device setup introduces user friction that NordPass avoids with its streamlined recovery key setup.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can NordPass or 1Password see my passwords?
No. Both services utilize strict zero-knowledge architecture. Encryption and decryption occur locally on your device using keys derived from your Master Password. Neither company holds the keys, meaning even under a court subpoena or internal rogue employee attempt, your data cannot be decrypted.

Yes. NordPass includes a built-in one-click 1Password import tool. Simply export your 1Password vault into a standard .1pux or .csv file and drag it into NordPass. All your logins, notes, passkeys, and credit card details transfer in seconds.
